Why We Built Horalix: Verifiable AI Echocardiography Workflow Software

NEWS | August 1, 2024 | Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina

Horalix was founded in Sarajevo to make echocardiography reporting faster, more structured, and easier for clinicians to verify.

Short answer

Horalix was founded in Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina, to make echocardiography analysis and reporting faster, more structured, and easier for clinicians to verify.

The company is building AI-assisted workflow software that processes cardiac ultrasound studies, extracts structured measurements, and prepares report-ready information while keeping physicians responsible for interpretation and final approval.

What is Horalix?

Horalix is a healthcare technology company developing artificial intelligence software for clinical imaging workflows.

Its first major product focus is echocardiography: the use of ultrasound to examine the structure, movement, valves, chambers, and function of the heart.

Horalix is not designed as an autonomous replacement for cardiologists. It is being developed as a workflow-support platform that helps clinical teams complete suitable measurement, review, and reporting tasks more efficiently.

The problem Horalix is solving

An echocardiography examination does not end when the cardiac ultrasound images are captured.

After acquisition, cardiologists and sonographers may still need to:

  • Identify relevant cardiac views
  • Review multiple video clips
  • Perform manual measurements
  • Verify chamber dimensions and cardiac function
  • Compare findings across images
  • Organize results into a structured report
  • Complete clinical interpretation and sign-off

Each step is important, but parts of the workflow are repetitive and suitable for carefully designed automation.

Horalix focuses on this post-acquisition workflow.

Why echocardiography first?

Horalix began with echocardiography because it combines:

  • High clinical importance
  • Rich visual and temporal information
  • Numerous structured measurements
  • Repetitive post-scan work
  • A clear need for clinician verification
  • Established DICOM imaging workflows
  • Opportunities for more consistent reporting

This makes cardiac ultrasound a strong starting point for responsible clinical workflow automation.

Rather than building a generic artificial intelligence dashboard, Horalix chose a specific workflow where software can provide practical, measurable support.

What Horalix is building

The Horalix CardiologyAI platform is designed to assist with the processing, analysis, review, and reporting of echocardiography studies.

The platform brings together:

  • DICOM study processing
  • Cardiac view analysis
  • Structured measurement extraction
  • Image and video review
  • Clinician-verifiable outputs
  • Report preparation
  • Secure user and study management
  • Deployment within healthcare environments

Horalix’s internal product benchmarks and measurement scope are disclosed separately on the company’s evidence and benchmark page.

Why verification matters

A prediction alone is not enough in medicine.

Clinicians need to understand:

  • Which study produced the output
  • Which image or video clip supports it
  • Whether the measurement is clinically plausible
  • Whether image quality affected the result
  • Whether the output should be accepted, corrected, or rejected

For this reason, Horalix is designed around human verification.

Artificial intelligence may assist with processing and structure, but qualified medical professionals remain responsible for interpretation and patient-care decisions.

Clinical AI should reduce cognitive load

The founding principle behind Horalix is that clinical software should make work calmer, not more complicated.

A useful platform should not create another disconnected dashboard that forces clinicians to repeat the same work in a different interface.

It should help teams:

  • Reach report-ready information faster
  • Review measurements in context
  • Reduce repetitive manual interactions
  • Maintain consistent documentation
  • Preserve clinical control
  • Integrate with existing imaging workflows

The technology must adapt to the clinical process rather than expecting the clinical process to adapt entirely to the technology.

What Horalix does not claim

Horalix does not claim that artificial intelligence can replace cardiologists or independently manage patient care.

The company also does not treat external research results as direct proof of Horalix product performance.

Horalix distinguishes between:

  • Internal product benchmarks
  • Clinical workflow observations
  • Hospital and clinic pilot feedback
  • Independent external research
  • Formal clinical evidence
  • Regulatory authorization

This separation is part of the company’s commitment to transparent healthcare communication. Full details are available through the Horalix evidence policy.

Building healthcare technology from Sarajevo

Horalix was founded with the belief that a specialized medical technology company can be built from Bosnia and Herzegovina for hospitals and clinical teams across Europe and other international markets.

Sarajevo provides access to engineering talent, healthcare professionals, universities, startup programs, and a growing regional innovation ecosystem.

The company’s ambition is not only to develop artificial intelligence models. It is to build dependable clinical software that can operate across different hospitals, imaging systems, and healthcare environments.

What is the long-term Horalix vision?

Horalix aims to build clinical AI infrastructure that helps healthcare teams process medical information more efficiently while preserving human responsibility.

Echocardiography is the starting point.

The broader direction includes:

  • More structured clinical imaging workflows
  • Better connection between AI output and source evidence
  • Secure hospital deployment
  • Interoperability with healthcare systems
  • Responsible clinical decision support
  • Tools that help healthcare expertise scale

The central principle will remain the same:

Artificial intelligence should support clinical judgment, not obscure or replace it.
